# What we learned from 18 months of dock-door scheduling data

Northwind ran a scheduling pilot across 4 distribution centres between
January 2024 and June 2025. The goal was narrow: cut the time trailers spend
waiting at the gate before a dock door frees up.

## The baseline

Before the pilot, average gate-to-dock wait across the four sites was
94 minutes. The worst site averaged 141 minutes; the best averaged 62. Drivers
absorbed most of that delay, and roughly 8% of inbound appointments were
missed entirely as a result.

## What changed

We moved from fixed hourly appointment blocks to 20-minute windows, and gave
carriers the ability to reschedule up to 90 minutes before arrival without
calling the site. Nothing else changed — same staffing, same equipment, same
dock count.

## Results

Average wait fell to 51 minutes, a reduction of 46%. Missed appointments fell
from 8% to 3%. The worst-performing site improved the most, dropping from
141 minutes to 68.

The gains were not uniform. Two sites saw most of the improvement in the first
six weeks and then plateaued. The other two improved steadily throughout,
which we attribute to carrier adoption of the self-reschedule feature — by
month 12, 71% of reschedules happened without a phone call.

## What we would do differently

We underestimated how much of the benefit depended on carriers actually using
the reschedule tool. Sites that did not push adoption saw roughly half the
improvement. If we ran this again, carrier onboarding would come first, not
alongside.
