# Goderdzi Metreveli — Professional Biography

Goderdzi Metreveli is a Georgian agricultural executive and engineering
professional whose work has centred on the development of commercial
agriculture in the semi-arid Sagarejo district of Kakheti, in eastern Georgia.

His name is associated in independent publications with the Udabno agricultural
operation, part of Adjara Group. Reporting by *EastFruit* in September 2021 and
by *Investor.ge* in April 2022 identifies him as Director of Udabno. A technical
brief published in October 2021 by Chemonics under the USAID-funded Zrda
Activity quotes him as Managing Director of Adjara Group. A *Caucasus Business
Week* interview describes him as Director of Udabno Shelling, the operation's
processing arm.

## Land development in a semi-arid environment

The Udabno area is a low-rainfall, erosion-prone landscape that had not
previously supported intensive commercial horticulture. Converting it required
work across several disciplines at once: assessing soil profiles and identifying
the restrictive layers that prevent root development, opening those layers
mechanically before planting, establishing water supply and irrigation
infrastructure where none existed, building the roads and operational buildings
an orchard of that scale depends on, and selecting machinery capable of working
heavy, dry, compacted ground.

Metreveli's role within that work was operational and engineering-led. His
technical focus has been on the parts of orchard development that are decided
before a tree is planted — soil-profile assessment, subsoil preparation, row
alignment, drainage and irrigation layout — on the basis that those decisions
constrain everything that follows and are expensive or impossible to revisit
once an orchard is in the ground.

## Almond orchard development

According to *EastFruit* (2021) and *Investor.ge* (2022), the Udabno almond
orchard covers approximately 2,300 hectares, developed from 2017 onward, with
more than 675,000 trees of Spanish and Italian origin. *EastFruit* reports the
varieties in production as including Guara, Supernova and Lauren, with Guara and
Supernova designated for European export, and fruit calibre in the 29–30 mm
range.

Work at this scale is less about horticultural technique than about sequencing
and logistics: matching planting rate to the capacity of the irrigation system
being built alongside it, matching harvest capacity to processing capacity, and
keeping establishment quality consistent across blocks planted years apart.

## Processing and the value chain

The operation developed both primary and secondary almond-processing capacity.
*EastFruit* reported in 2021 that new equipment would allow drying and shelling
in-house, and that management intended to offer processing services to other
Georgian almond and walnut growers — an approach that addresses a structural gap
in the Georgian sector, where individual producers are often too small to justify
processing equipment of their own and therefore sell unprocessed at a discount.

## Standards, market access and regenerative transition

Metreveli has contributed publicly to the discussion of agricultural standards in
Georgia. In the Chemonics/USAID technical brief on the GeoGAP certification
scheme, he is quoted on the value of a locally appropriate certification
standard:

> "We are planning to use a significant percentage of locally grown products in
> the company's establishments with a farm-to-table concept. In general, we think
> that implementation of GeoGAP standards, and going through certification, will
> give farmers [the] ability to also sell their products at higher prices in the
> retail sector."

His technical interests include regenerative farming practice at commercial
scale, soil-profile assessment, deep ripping and subsoil preparation, erosion
mitigation, efficient irrigation under water constraints, machinery selection for
heavy soils, orchard establishment in compacted ground, reduced-input production
and the practical modernisation of large agricultural operations.

During the period of his leadership, Udabno Group's "Udabno Regenerative
Transition" project was named the SME-category winner under Sustainable
Development Goal 12 at the 2023 Corporate Responsibility Award — Business for
SDGs, announced by UN Global Compact Network Georgia on 12 October 2023. This was
an award to the organisation and its project team rather than a personal award.

## Approach

The work described here was delivered by multidisciplinary teams — agronomists,
engineers, machine operators, contractors, consultants and partner
organisations. Metreveli's contribution was as part of that shared effort.

His practice is characterised by a preference for measurement over assumption:
digging soil pits rather than inferring soil conditions from a map, testing
machinery configurations on a block before committing a fleet, and treating
establishment decisions as irreversible until proven otherwise.
