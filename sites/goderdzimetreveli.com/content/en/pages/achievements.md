## 1. Semi-arid land development

Udabno, in the Sagarejo district of Kakheti, is low-rainfall, erosion-prone land
that had not previously supported intensive commercial horticulture. From 2017
onward it was developed into a working agricultural operation.

I held a senior leadership role within that operation — identified in
independent reporting as its Director during 2021–2022. The operation is publicly
reported as controlling or managing a substantial landholding, and I want to be
precise about that figure because published accounts do not agree:

| Source | Figure |
|---|---|
| *Caucasus Business Week* interview (self-reported) | approximately 20,000 hectares of farmland |
| Chemonics / USAID brief, 2021 | "more than 4,000 hectares of land" |
| UN Global Compact Network Georgia | over 5,000 hectares of agricultural land invested in since 2017 |

`[VERIFICATION REQUIRED: land-registry or audited company records separating
land owned, leased and under active cultivation]`

Until that is resolved, no total-area figure appears in the headline claims on
this site. The likeliest explanation is that these figures describe different
things — total territory, land acquired, and land under cultivation — but until a
document confirms which is which, publishing the largest number would be
misleading.

What the work involved is not in dispute: land preparation and subsoil opening,
water-supply and irrigation infrastructure built where none existed, orchard
establishment, annual crop production, soil protection and erosion mitigation,
farm roads and operational buildings, and agricultural mechanization suited to
heavy dry ground.

**No individual transformed this land.** It was done by multidisciplinary teams
of agronomists, engineers, operators, contractors and partner organisations.

## 2. Large-scale almond orchard development

*EastFruit* (September 2021) and *Investor.ge* (April 2022) both report the
Udabno almond orchard at approximately **2,300 hectares**, developed from 2017,
with more than **675,000 trees** of Spanish and Italian origin.

*EastFruit* reports varieties including **Guara, Supernova and Lauren**, with
Guara and Supernova designated for European export, and fruit calibre of
**29–30 mm**.

Work covered orchard planning and block layout, variety selection where
documented, soil preparation before planting, irrigation design and installation,
harvest planning, machinery specification, organic-transition objectives, and
preparation for European market requirements.

The consistent difficulty at this scale is matching rates. Planting can outrun
irrigation construction; harvest capacity can outrun processing capacity;
establishment quality can drift between blocks planted years apart. Most of the
management effort goes into keeping those in step rather than into any single
technical decision.

## 3. Almond processing and value-chain development

The operation developed both primary and secondary almond-processing capacity.
*EastFruit* reported in 2021 that new equipment would enable drying and shelling
in-house, and that management intended to offer processing services to other
Georgian almond and walnut growers — with a view to a regional processing base.

That intention matters more than it might appear. Most Georgian nut producers are
too small to justify their own processing equipment, so they sell unprocessed and
take the thinnest margin in the chain. Accessible regional processing changes
what an independent grower's crop is worth.

**Figures held back.** My professional record includes a processing investment of
approximately USD 15 million, capacity of approximately 14–15 tonnes per hour,
and a named equipment manufacturer. None is published here:

- The only published capacity figure is **2.5 tonnes per hour** (*EastFruit*,
  2021) — roughly a sixth of the figure in my record. The two may measure
  different things: in-hull intake versus kernel throughput, or an original line
  versus a later expansion. Until a specification sheet says which,
  neither figure should be presented as the site's own.
- No published source isolates processing capital expenditure. *Investor.ge*
  reports GEL 150 million invested in the Udabno operation as a whole, which is
  a different quantity.
- Sources spell the equipment manufacturer differently, and two real
  manufacturers match the different spellings.

## 4. European export preparation

*EastFruit* reports that Guara and Supernova plantings were designated for
European export, and that the operation was developing export-oriented
production. A *Caucasus Business Week* interview refers to Europe and Gulf
countries as target markets.

**What is claimed here:** export-oriented production developed and targeted at
European markets, with variety selection made accordingly.

**What is not claimed here:** completed and audited export shipments. Harvest
forecasts are not production results, and the two published figures for 2021
illustrate why — *EastFruit* reported a forecast of 500–600 tonnes in September
2021, while *Investor.ge* reported an 800-tonne in-shell crop the following
April. Forecast and outcome are separate facts and are not merged on this site.

## 5. Regenerative transition and eco-innovation

Work in this area covered reduced environmental impact, organic-transition
planning, irrigation efficiency under constrained water supply, improved nutrient
management, erosion mitigation, lower chemical-input objectives, zero-waste and
circular-production concepts, and — the constraint that makes the rest difficult
— commercial viability throughout.

Regenerative practice at commercial scale is not the same discipline as
regenerative practice on a smallholding. The techniques are similar; the
constraints are not. A 2,000-hectare orchard cannot adopt a practice that halves
throughput during the harvest window, however good it is for the soil, and a
practice that cannot survive a bad year will not survive at all.

> **Udabno Group's "Udabno Regenerative Transition" project was named the
> SME-category winner under Sustainable Development Goal 12 at the 2023
> Corporate Responsibility Award — Business for SDGs, announced by UN Global
> Compact Network Georgia on 12 October 2023.**

This was an award **to the organisation and its project team**, made during the
period of my leadership. It was not a personal award. The same announcement names
individual honourees separately under its SDG Pioneer category, and I was not
among them.

The ceremony was held at the Rustaveli Theatre with support from the Government
of Sweden, USAID's Civil Society Engagement Program and the Council of Europe
Office; entries were judged in large-business and SME categories by a jury of
Georgian and international members.

**On organic certification:** this site does not claim certified organic
production. *EastFruit* reported in 2021 that the company *planned* transition to
certified organic production "in a few years"; a *Caucasus Business Week*
interview records my own description of the growing method as using "no
preparations, whether organic or otherwise". "Organic" is a legally protected
term in both Georgian and EU marketing law, and it is not used here without a
certificate.

## 6. GeoGAP and market standards

The Chemonics/USAID Zrda Activity technical brief on GeoGAP (October 2021) quotes
me twice by name and title on the value of the scheme:

> "Now, when buying GeoGAP-certified products, we have a guarantee that they are
> locally grown, safe (in terms of chemical and biological contamination),
> traceable, and environmentally friendly."

The same brief records that Adjara Group planned to implement good agricultural
practices and earn GeoGAP certification at its almond orchard, berry farm and
vineyard. That is documented as a **plan**; this site does not claim the
certification was achieved.

GeoGAP was created by the Georgian Farmers' Association with USAID and Chemonics
support as a scaled-down, locally calibrated stepping-stone toward international
schemes such as GLOBALG.A.P. Its value is precisely that it is achievable: an
export-grade scheme that no domestic producer can afford does nothing for the
domestic market.

## A note on how this page is written

Achievements attributed to an organisation are described as organisational.
Figures carry sources. Contradictions between sources are shown rather than
resolved silently. Items lacking documentation are marked and withheld.

This makes for a less emphatic page than the alternative. It also makes it one
that holds up when checked — which, for a professional record, is the only
property that matters.
